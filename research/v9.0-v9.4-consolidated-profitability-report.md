## Main result: 21 frozen historical market–engine combinations

Reused the **verified** v7.6 six-month (April–September 2026) historical 5m Binance USD-M archive summary, not new or independent forward observations. Original 18 archives contained 158,112 market-specific five-minute candles. v9.0's [actual source data break-even audit](https://github.com/Elikers/invo-ultimate/actions/runs/44223634390) passed all three unit checks and validated 42 original 8/16-bps market-engine cost records, consistent to within float tolerance.

The decisive result across 21 market-engine groups, using trade-count weighted **per-month final 25% retrospective folds**, and 8 bps hypothetical round-trip costs:
- **14 of 21** had *negative or zero* inferred gross average return **even with hypothetical zero cost**.
- **7 of 21** had *positive simulated gross average return* but **not enough to cover an 8-bps round trip**.
- **0 of 21** had positive net historical weighted mean after 8 bps (also 0 at 16, 40, 78 bps fixed-cost re-pricing in v8.1).

The gross-positive, cost-negative groups were **BTC sweep, BTC mean, ETH sweep, ETH auction, ETH mean, SOL auction, SOL mean**. These are **retrospective findings**, with multiple comparison bias; **none is a forward-validated profitable strategy**. Reporting them is diagnostic, not candidate selection or production recommendation.

Mathematics: `gross inferred mean % = historical mean net % at 8bps + 0.08%`; `break_even_cost_bps = gross inferred mean % × 100`. A negative break-even figure means the average simulated gross edge is itself negative, not that negative fees could be realized. These identities assume the same hypothetical fills and do not model spread/impact/stop-fill feedback.
